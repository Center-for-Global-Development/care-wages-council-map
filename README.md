# What higher care wages would cost councils: interactive figures

Six figures for CGD's blog on care wages and council budgets (numbered as in the blog):
- **Figure 1, `care-wage-workforce-map.html`:** how much each council's care workforce relies on non-EU workers, with vacancy rates.
- **Figure 2, `care-wage-pay-premium.html`:** care pay above the minimum wage, the vacancy rate, care visas and the change in posts filled by British workers, 2018/19 to 2025/26.
- **Figure 3, `care-wage-budget-lines.html`:** the England-wide extra cost of higher care wages compared with other council budget lines.
- **Figure 4, `care-wage-fiscal-breakeven.html`:** care workers' cumulative savings to the public finances, by source, against the MAC's net lifetime cost.
- **Figure 5, `care-wage-council-map.html`:** the cost to each council, as a map.
- **Figure 6, `care-wage-settlement-timing.html`:** when care workers on care visas reach five years and can apply for settlement.

All use the same shared layer; Figures 3 and 5 share the five benchmark buttons. The sections below describe the cost map first; the others have their own sections further down. Some older section headings use earlier numbering: "Figure 1: budget lines" is now Figure 3, "Figure 2: council map" is Figure 5, and "Figure 4: when care workers ... reach settlement" is Figure 6. Since 8 Nov 2026 the break-even chart is Figure 4.

## Figure 2: council map

An interactive companion to CGD's blog on care wages and council budgets. The reader picks how much higher care labour costs are, using one of five benchmarks (4.5, 9, 12, 15 or 20%). Each English council is then shaded either by the council tax rise needed to cover the extra yearly cost (the cost as a share of its council tax requirement, assuming the full cost is passed on through council tax) or by the cost as a share of its core spending power.

Hovering or tapping a council shows:
- its extra cost, and that cost as a share of council tax and of spending power;
- the extra cost per dwelling;
- the extra cost compared with its library spending;
- its budget line closest in size to the extra cost.

Selecting a council lists the extra cost against six of its net service budgets.

The figure is built for a cgdev.org iframe and follows the CGD interactive-visualisation standard.

- **Question:** if care labour costs had to rise by X%, how big would the extra cost be for each council, relative to its own budget?
- **Default view:** 9% (MAC: £1 above the minimum wage), shaded by council tax rise needed, with no council selected. The side panel shows England totals.

## Regenerating the data

The data comes from the analysis pipeline in the project folder (OneDrive: `UK healthcare visas/scripts`). It uses the same model as the blog and workbook (`14_wage_uplift.py`). Cost is linear in the uplift, so the page stores each council's cost per percentage point.

```
python scripts/18_interactive_map_data.py
```

`run_all.py` runs this step and writes straight into this repository's `data/` folder. Commit and push afterwards to update the published map.

The script prints the England cost at 9% (£1,533.9m, matching the workbook) and a join audit. All 151 councils match the boundaries. City of London has boundaries but no data and is hatched as "not included". The Isles of Scilly take Cornwall's values.

## Method (summarised in the figure's notes)

- **Cost:** a uniform increase to the labour share of commissioned adult social care spending (DHSC ASC-FR 2025-26), passed in full into fees. The labour shares are 60% for care homes, 75% for community care and 70% for short-term care.
- **Denominators:**
  - each council's own 2025/26 council tax requirement (MHCLG Table 10);
  - core spending power 2025/26;
  - dwellings.
- **Budget lines:** 2025/26 net revenue spending (revenue outturn), using 2024/25 where a council has not yet reported. Lines with zero or negative net spending show as "–". A council with none (Cumberland) shows a short message instead.
- **Hourly pay:** £12.60 × (1 + increase), where £12.60 is Skills for Care's March 2026 median.
- **How pay rises across councils** (same definitions as `14_wage_uplift.py`):
  - *Uniform* (default): every council's care labour costs rise by the selected increase.
  - *Pay floor*: a national floor of £12.60 × (1 + increase). Each council's rise is max(0, floor ÷ its median care worker pay − 1), using Skills for Care's 2025/26 council medians. At 9% this costs £1.18bn rather than £1.53bn, and 26 councils (not 51) would need more than a 4.99% council tax rise.
  - *Market-led*: rise = increase × (0.5 + 0.5 × council non-EU share ÷ the labour-cost-weighted mean share), so the England total equals uniform but the rise is larger where reliance on non-EU workers is higher.
  - When the variant isn't uniform, the side panel subtitle and the tooltip give the council's own rise.
- **Projection:** Lambert azimuthal equal-area, centred on England.
- **Context land:** Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland are drawn in dim grey from ONS Countries (December 2024, BUC), so their borders match the council boundaries. Ireland, the Isle of Man and the French coast come from Natural Earth 1:50m (world-atlas 2.0.2). They are not interactive and are drawn at 60% opacity. The view is widened slightly on both sides (70 units west, 60 east), so England sits centred and the London inset sits over the Irish Sea.

## Design decisions (settled; change only deliberately)

- **One screen:** on screens wider than 600px the map sits on the left, with the controls and the detail panel stacked on the right. The whole figure is about 745px tall at desktop widths, so it fits on one laptop screen without scrolling. On phones it stacks.
- **Benchmarks:** the increase is chosen from the blog's five benchmarks only (there is no free slider). Each button's quick tip names its source.
- **Colour bins** are fixed for each measure and held across benchmarks, so a change of colour always means a change of cost.
  - Council tax: <2.5, 2.5–5, 5–7.5, 7.5–10, 10–15 and 15+. The break at 5% is close to a year's maximum rise (4.99%).
  - Spending power: <1, 1–2, 2–3, 3–4, 4–6 and 6+.
- **Library comparison** uses net spending, consistent with the other budget lines. The analysis workbook's `x_library_gross` uses gross spending.
- **Notes** are set at 9.6px (below the house 10.8px) at the author's request, with linked sources.
- **No data download.**
- **Measure labels:** the toggle reads "Show the cost as: Council tax rise | % of spending power". The council tax measure is labelled "council tax rise needed" throughout, and the footnote states the full pass-through assumption.
- **Colour key:** the legend title sits on its own line, and the bin labels have a fixed width, so switching measure never reflows the figure.
- **Keyboard route:** councils are not tab stops. The keyboard route is the "Find a council" select, and the side panel is the text alternative.
- **Council finder placement:** on the cost map the "Find a council" select sits in the map header, beside the legend title, to make room for the variant toggle within one screen. Verified 3 Nov 2026: 750px for England and at most 759px with any council selected, across all variants and both measures at 1280px; layout checks at 320, 390 and 700px clean.

## Figure 1 (blog numbering): reliance on non-EU care workers

`care-wage-workforce-map.html` (`data-cgd-interactive-name="care-wages-workforce-map"`): https://center-for-global-development.github.io/care-wages-council-map/care-wage-workforce-map.html

- **Question:** how much does each council's independent care workforce rely on non-EU nationals?
- **Default:** the share of care worker posts held by non-EU nationals (shading).
- **Views:** a toggle switches to the number of such posts, shown as circles sized by count. Circles rather than shading, because shading raw counts would mainly show council size.
- **Vacancies view:** shades councils by the care worker vacancy rate (bins <5, 5–7.5, 7.5–10, 10–12.5, 12.5–15 and 15+%), with a year switch for 2025/26 or 2021/22. Vacancies = rate × filled posts ÷ (1 − rate). England: 12.4% (about 116,000) in 2021/22, 7.2% (about 70,000) in 2025/26. 2021/22 rates are missing for four councils (hatched).
- **Tooltip and side panel** for each council:
  - the share, its rank and the regional average;
  - estimated non-EU posts and all care worker posts;
  - the 2021/22 share.
- **Workforce only:** the map deliberately carries no cost or council tax figures, which the blog introduces later.
- **Data** (`data/workforce-data.js`, written by `18_interactive_map_data.py`): Skills for Care local-area data 2025/26, independent sector, care worker job role.
  - Non-EU posts = filled posts × non-EU share, rounded.
  - Nationally: 38% (about 338,000 posts), up from 12% in 2021/22.
  - Circle positions are each council's mainland centroid. London's circles sit in the inset.

## Figure 4: when care workers recruited from abroad reach settlement

`care-wage-settlement-timing.html` (`data-cgd-interactive-name="care-wages-settlement-timing"`)

- **Now Figure 6 in the blog.** Updated 5 Nov 2026 to stack in-country switchers (see "In-country switchers" below).
- **Question:** when do the care workers recruited on care visas in 2021–25 reach five years on the visa, the point at which they can apply for settlement and are free to leave the sector?
- **Data** (`data/settlement-timing-data.js`, written by `18_interactive_map_data.py`; run `python scripts/18_interactive_map_data.py --timing-only` to refresh just this file): Home Office sponsored work visas by occupation (SOC 2020), year ending June 2026. Health and Care Worker entry clearance grants to main applicants in caring personal services (SOC 613), each placed five years after the quarter of grant.
  - Only grants before the 12 May 2025 White Paper are kept: later entrants face a 10-year route under the 2 Nov 2026 decision. 2025 Q2 is split pro rata by days (41 of 91).
  - About 160,000 in all: 39,000 from now to end 2027, 108,000 during 2028 (peak 2028 Q3, 34,500), 12,000 from 2029.
- **Controls:**
  - *Show by* quarter or year.
  - *Count*: all visas granted, or "expected to qualify", which applies the Home Office's indicative 146,000 of 196,000 (about three-quarters) to every period.
- **Past and future:** quarters before the current calendar quarter are pale (not labelled in the key, at the author's request; the "Now" line marks the boundary), computed from the viewer's date, so the chart stays current. The year view stacks the passed and to-come parts of each year.
- **No subtitle or summary cards** (removed 5 Nov 2026 at the author's request): the title states the finding, and the notes say which visas are counted. The period totals are in the blog text and the tooltips.
- **Markers:** a "Now" line in the quarter view; a shaded 2028 band (in-country switching into care visas ends in July 2028; the first Fair Pay Agreement is due).
- **Fixed axes:** 45,000 for quarters and 150,000 for years, held across the count toggle so switching shrinks the bars rather than rescaling.
- **Limitations (in the notes):** excludes in-country switchers (the Home Office's 196,000 includes them); includes some who have since left; grant date stands in for entry date; reaching five years is not the same as leaving care.
- **Verification (5 Nov 2026, after removing the subtitle and cards):** 653px tall at 1280px in all four views; at 320–375px the 2028 band label sits left of the band; no overflow or console errors.

### In-country switchers (Figures 2 and 5)

- **Source:** Home Office FOI 03109, published by the Migration Observatory as the underlying data for "International students entering the UK labour market" (2026). It gives certificates of sponsorship used for care worker and senior care worker roles, by quarter (2021 Q1 to 2026 Q1), split into out-of-country applications and in-country switchers from the Sponsored Study route.
- **Cross-check:** the FOI out-of-country series matches the published entry-clearance grants closely (for example 34,301 vs 34,513 in 2023 Q3).
- **Totals:** about 52,100 study-to-care switches in all; 50,400 before the White Paper cut-off. With the 160,000 granted from abroad, that makes about 210,000 reaching five years: about 55,000 from now to end 2027, 132,000 during 2028 and 23,000 from 2029. The Home Office's 196,000 due to settle in 2026–30 is net of departures and includes other switchers.
- **Settlement clock:** time on a student visa doesn't count towards Skilled Worker settlement, so switchers are placed five years after the switch. The 2 Nov 2026 decision covers care workers who "entered, or switched into" the route before May 2025.
- **Not included:** switchers from the Graduate route (the Migration Observatory puts these at around 26,000 in the year to June 2023 alone, from a separate FOI), dependants and other in-country routes. Both figures therefore understate in-country recruitment.

## Figure 4: care workers' fiscal savings against the MAC's estimate

`care-wage-fiscal-breakeven.html` (`data-cgd-interactive-name="care-wages-fiscal-breakeven"`), added 8 Nov 2026.

- **Question:** how many years of care work does it take for the savings the MAC's lifetime estimate leaves out to match its net cost (about £81,000 per care worker, including the share of adult dependants)?
- **Chart:** stacked areas of cumulative savings per care worker, in present value at 3% (the MAC's rate): lower care wage bill (net of the 40% the Treasury recovers and of recruits' tax and benefits forgone), family carers kept in work, fewer delayed discharges, less A&E use. A dashed line marks the MAC cost, a dot the break-even year, and a dotted line the expected time in care.
- **Controls:** *Assumptions* (Cautious, Central, Generous) and *Without international workers, care posts would be* (Filled by higher pay, Partly filled, Left unfilled: θ = 1, 0.5, 0). Default: Central, partly filled (break-even 7.2 years).
- **Axes fixed** at 0–30 years and £0–200k in every state, so only the assumptions move the picture; areas above £200k are clipped. Where break-even is beyond 30 years, the label says so.
- **Interaction:** hover, tap or arrow keys on the plot show cumulative savings by source for that year and the share of the MAC cost.
- **Method and sources:** 'Methodology note - care worker fiscal spillovers.docx' and 'Fiscal ledger scenarios.xlsx' in the project folder.
- **Verification (8 Nov 2026):** 665px tall at 1280px; at 375px the counterfactual buttons use short labels; no overflow or console errors.

## Figure 4 update (9 Nov 2026): same price basis as the MAC

Savings are now converted from 2025/26 to 2022/23 prices (HM Treasury GDP deflator, Sept 2026: 0.8846) and grown at 1.8% a year in real terms (the MAC's baseline growth in spending and taxes) before discounting at 3%, so they match the MAC's present value. `data/fiscal-breakeven-data.js` now carries `g` and `defl`; cumulative savings = annual × defl × (1 − ρ^t)/(r − g), with ρ = (1 + g)/(1 + r). Central years to net fiscally positive: 7.0 (posts unfilled) to 8.7 (filled by higher pay), 7.8 half and half; cautious 21–32; generous 2.9–5.8. The panel heading's annual figure stays in 2025/26 prices.

## Figure 4 update (9 Nov 2026, later): tenure and all routes

- **Expected time in care:** the cohort behind the MAC's figure is those expected to settle, and settling requires five years' sponsored work, so retention in years 1–5 is now 100%. After settlement, 25% a year leave (40% cautious, 15% generous). Expected years in care: 6.5 / 8.0 / 10.7.
- **Wage saving:** now covers the care councils buy, their own in-house services and direct payments (£210m per 1% uplift, was £170m for bought-in care only).
- **Attribution:** stated in the notes. The settling cohort is credited with the sector-wide effect, because international recruits supplied the marginal labour; dependants are not counted separately.
- **Result:** central 6.8–7.0 years to net fiscally positive (6.9 partly filled), against 8.0 expected years in care. Cautious 15–32; generous 2.9–4.7.
